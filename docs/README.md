# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management process documents used by OctoAcme. The purpose of these materials is to provide a lightweight but repeatable way to plan, execute, track, and improve work across delivery teams and stakeholders.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme uses a structured lifecycle that begins with initiation and moves through planning, execution, release, and retrospective. At the start of a project, teams define the problem, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, and high-level risks in a one-pager so the work has clear business purpose and sponsor alignment. Once approved, the team creates a prioritized backlog, clarifies acceptance criteria, estimates work, and identifies dependencies and milestones. This keeps delivery focused on customer value while making trade-offs and commitments visible.

The approach also defines clear roles and responsibilities. Product managers define priorities and outcomes, project managers coordinate schedules, communication, risks, and dependencies, developers build and validate the solution, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide direction and approvals. Communication is a deliberate part of the process: teams use weekly updates, standups, milestone reviews, and escalation paths so blockers and risks are surfaced early and resolved quickly. The process encourages transparency, cross-functional alignment, and a single source of truth for project status.

Quality is built into delivery rather than added at the end. OctoAcme expects unit, integration, and end-to-end smoke tests, CI validation, security scanning, and manual QA where needed to confirm that acceptance criteria are met. Pull requests are expected to be small and well-scoped, include issue references and acceptance criteria, and undergo review before merge. Release and deployment checklists, rollback planning, and post-release verification reduce operational risk and support a more reliable path from feature work to production.

At the end of each sprint or milestone, the team captures lessons learned through retrospectives and tracks improvement actions to close the loop. This continuous improvement mindset is reinforced through risk registers, weekly reviews, and action-item follow-up so the team can adapt quickly and improve its delivery process over time.
